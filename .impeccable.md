## Design Context

### Users
- **Who**: Spotify users (18-35) who care about their playlists — curators, collaborative playlist managers, music enthusiasts
- **Context**: Checking what changed in playlists, exploring analytics about their music taste, restoring accidentally deleted songs
- **Job to be done**: "I want to know what happened to my playlists and understand my music taste better"

### Brand Personality
- **3 words**: Warm, friendly, reliable
- **Voice**: Conversational, approachable, never technical or corporate. Like a music-loving friend who happens to be organized.
- **Emotional goal**: Delight & discovery — "This is fun to explore, I keep finding cool insights"

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Visual tone**: Clean, structured, elegant — inspired by Linear/Notion. Warm light theme with off-white/cream tones, not cold whites or dark mode.
- **Typography**: Space Grotesk (headings) + DM Sans (body) — modern geometric pair with warmth
- **Color**: Spotify green (#1DB954) as accent on warm off-white (#faf8f5) base. Cream/sand neutrals, never pure black or white.
- **References**: Linear (clean structure, elegant spacing), Notion (warm, friendly, organized), Apple Music (editorial, curated)
- **Anti-references**: Generic SaaS templates, developer/hacker tools (dark+monospace), overly corporate (stock photos, blue/gray)

### Design Principles
1. **Warm over cold** — Off-whites, cream tones, warm neutrals. Never clinical or sterile.
2. **Show, don't tell** — Demo tables, real album art, and visual examples beat marketing copy.
3. **Progressive disclosure** — Start simple, reveal depth through interaction. Don't overwhelm.
4. **Data is delightful** — Analytics and stats should feel like discovery, not spreadsheets.
5. **Music-native** — The interface should feel connected to music culture, not generic SaaS.
